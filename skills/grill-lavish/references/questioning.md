# Questioning discipline

Adapted from Matt Pocock's `grilling` workflow. See sources.md for attribution.

Treat the plan as a tree of decisions. Ask all unresolved questions whose
prerequisites are settled in one round. A question dependent on an unanswered
question belongs to a later round. Prefer depth where answers reveal important
consequences, not arbitrary question counts.

State each decision, why it matters, concrete alternatives, and a recommendation
with its assumptions and trade-off. Recommendations are advice; never preselect
them or record them as the user's decision.

After each answer, reshape the tree. Challenge inconsistency politely, probe
failure cases, and surface missing stakeholders, scope boundaries, success
criteria, and irreversible choices. A prewritten checklist is not exhaustive.
Skip areas whose irrelevance is established by the user's scope.

Retrieve facts yourself using available tools. Record missing facts as research
prerequisites. Delegate independent research only when the harness permits it;
otherwise do it yourself. Ask for inaccessible organizational context honestly.

Keep unresolved decisions visible. Exclude a branch only for a reason grounded
in user-approved scope, never to avoid a difficult question. Do not promise that
every possible uncertainty has been eliminated.

Stop when the agreed scope has no unresolved decision or research prerequisite
and the user confirms shared understanding. Save progress when the user pauses.
Do not automatically begin building at the end of a review.
